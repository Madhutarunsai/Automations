Source: Fivos Aresti (Workflows.io) — "How I built a $250K/month business in 12 months"
Translated for: Tarun — solo, $5.5–8.5K/month, Upwork-primary, 365 IG followers
Goal this doc serves: $10K/month, with revenue that survives Upwork

**Verdict: the person and the company are real. The revenue number is marketing.**

### The thing most people miss about this video

This video *is* the funnel it describes. It's bottom-of-funnel content. The CTA at 19:14
("book a free call with us, first link in the description") is the entire point. The
90-day content plan in the description is the lead magnet.

That's not a reason to dismiss it. It's the single most useful thing in it:
**he is demonstrating the product by doing the product in front of you.** Teach the what,
sell the how. The video has low views and he explicitly says that's fine — because 200 of
the right viewers is worth more than 200,000 of the wrong ones at his deal size.

Copy that structure. Ignore the revenue number.

### Where the video is weak — don't copy these parts

1. **Massive survivorship omission.** He says "we started July 2025 at zero revenue" —
   then admits he had 16K followers and his co-founder had 24K, and that they'd been
   "planning for that moment for many months." They didn't start at zero. They started
   with 40K warm B2B buyers and a co-founder. The "12 months" is actually "12 months
   plus years of unpaid audience building."
2. **"We stayed flat Jan–June on purpose."** Six months of zero growth framed as a
   strategic choice. Maybe. Also exactly what a plateau sounds like when you narrate it
   afterwards.
3. **Ads are unproven and he says so.** 5–10K/month on LinkedIn ads with no results yet.
   He included the channel anyway because four channels sounds more like a system than
   three. Do not take ads advice from this video.
4. **6 emails in 3 days** after someone books a call is aggressive. He admits it annoys
   people. At your deal size it will annoy more of them.
5. **Tool stack is heavy:** Clay, Airtable, Calendly, Slack, HubSpot, HeyReach, Quillter,
   Arrows. That's a 10-person-company stack. It would eat your entire margin.

## PART 2 — WHAT HE'S ACTUALLY DOING

Strip the narrative and there are seven mechanics. Only these matter.

**1. Audience before offer.**
He built distribution first, then pointed it at a company. Everything else is downstream
of this. 70% of revenue comes from one channel: LinkedIn content.

**2. Give away the what, sell the how.**
Full teardowns, no gatekeeping. The buyer concludes "this person can clearly do this,"
and buys implementation. Trust is the product; the service is the invoice.

**3. One channel to saturation before adding a second.**
July 2025: content + manual outbound only. October: *same channels, higher cadence*
(2 posts/week → 4). January: 200K/month, and he explicitly says it was **not** new
channels — it was compounding. The discipline is in what he did *not* add.

**4. Turn every person you have into a distribution node.**
Two founders = 80K followers. The whole team = 160K. He doubled reach by paying his
team to build *their own* personal brands, ranked quarterly on a weighted score.
This is the highest-leverage idea in the video.

**5. Qualify automatically so volume doesn't kill you.**
100+ meetings/month. Form → captured → scored → disqualified or shown calendar →
pushed to Slack → one-click approve/reject. He never talks to an unqualified lead.

**6. Pre-frame before the call.**
Confirmation → what we do → biggest case study → why us → transformation → objections.
The call starts with the prospect already sold; the call just handles logistics.

**7. Funnel-aware content, not just "content."**
- **TOF** (the SF AI company map): generic, broad, gets eyeballs and followers.
- **MOF**: simplified explanations, gets the right accounts to engage.
- **BOF**: deep teardowns with a CTA, converts.
He runs all three simultaneously and knows which post is doing which job.

## PART 3 — WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER TO YOU

Be honest about the gap before building anything.

| He has | You have |
|---|---|
| 40K follower head start on LinkedIn | 365 followers on Instagram |
| A co-founder with his own 24K audience | Yourself |
| A team of ~10 to turn into a media network | Yourself |
| $5–10K/month for ad testing | ~$500–800/month total tool spend |
| 100+ inbound meetings/month | Upwork's marketplace sending you work |
| Enterprise deal sizes (Alibaba, Nvidia) | $1,200–$6,000 projects |

**Four of his seven mechanics assume a team or an existing audience.** Copying the video
literally means spending 12 months building an audience before earning anything — and you
already have revenue to protect.

### But here's the part that *does* transfer, and it's the important one

His entire thesis is: **own your distribution**. Don't rent it.

You currently rent 100% of yours from Upwork. 16 projects, 100% JSS, Top Rated Plus — all
of it is equity in someone else's platform. If Upwork changes its algorithm or suspends
your account tomorrow, your revenue is zero. That is the actual lesson of this video for
you, and it has nothing to do with LinkedIn.

The goal is not "become Fivos." The goal is: **by month 12, less than 50% of revenue
comes from Upwork.**

## PART 4 — YOUR VERSION OF THE FOUR CHANNELS

### Channel 0 (he never mentions it): protect the cash

Upwork stays. It funds everything else. Do not reduce proposal volume to chase content.
Every new channel below is built on top of a stable Upwork base, never instead of it.

**Rule: 70% of working hours on paid client work, 30% on owned distribution.** Not 50/50.
You cannot afford a dip.

---

### Channel 1 — Content, but on the platform where your buyers are

**Problem:** your buyers are staffing firms, agencies, and B2B operators in LA / Texas /
Washington. They are on **LinkedIn**. You have 365 followers on **Instagram**.

Instagram is where freelancers who want to *be* you hang out. LinkedIn is where people
who want to *hire* you hang out. One builds an audience, the other builds a pipeline.

**Do this:**
- LinkedIn becomes the primary channel. Instagram continues, costs nothing extra —
  it's the same content repurposed (exactly how he treats X).
- **2 posts/week for 8 weeks, then 3, then 4.** His exact ramp. Don't start at 5.
- Write every post yourself. He still does, at $250K/month, because it's the highest-ROI
  hour in the business. Delegate the graphic, never the copy.
- Funnel mix per week at 3 posts: 1 TOF, 1 MOF, 1 BOF.

**Your unfair advantage over him:** he has to pay a ghostwriting team. You already have,
in this repo:
- `content_scripts/ALL_60_SCRIPTS.md` — 60 written scripts
- `content_scripts/FRAMEWORK.md` — hook and structure rules
- `content_scripts/KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` + `REAL_DATA.md` — source of truth
- `content_scripts/CONTENT_AUDIT.md` — what never to post
- `blog_engine/` — 5 SEO/AEO/GEO posts per day, automated
- `lead_magnets/` — 16 built, PDFs and HTML done

**This repo is your version of his 10-person team.** He bought reach with salaries and
quarterly prize money. You buy it with automation you already wrote. That's the single
realistic substitute for mechanic #4.

**What to post that he can't:** he has case studies. You have a *story* — $849/month
teacher in Chiang Rai to a real automation business. Per `CONTENT_AUDIT.md`, lead with the
reverse-engineering stories, the speed results, the artist/musician angle. Keep the scam,
the borrowed money, and the follower count off the page.

---

### Channel 2 — Manual outbound (START HERE, this week)

**This is the only channel in the video that works at zero audience.** It's also the one
he started with, and the one he still hasn't automated for himself after 12 months.

His "Dream 150." Your version — **Dream 50**, because you deliver alone:

1. Take your 2 best clients (a staffing firm, the Texas metal company) and reverse-engineer
   the pattern:
   - **Firmographic:** industry, headcount, location
   - **Technographic:** do they run GoHighLevel, Airtable, ClickUp, n8n?
   - **Signals:** hiring for ops/recruiting roles, posting about manual processes,
     recently funded, running job ads at volume
2. Build the list — Apollo is enough. You don't need Clay yet.
3. Find the stakeholders. For staffing firms: owner, Head of Recruiting, Ops Manager.
4. **60 minutes a day. Calendar block. Non-negotiable.** 3 minutes per contact, ~20/day,
   ~100/week.
5. Personalize on something AI could not have written. This is his one actual rule and
   it's correct.

**Why this beats content for you right now:** content pays in month 6. Outbound pays in
week 3. You need both, but outbound funds the patience that content requires.

**Do not automate this until it works manually.** He didn't, and he says so at 18:51 —
automating their own outbound is still only a *plan* at $250K/month.

### Channel 3 — Partnerships (your highest-leverage channel, and he undersells it)

He frames partnerships as co-marketing with thought leaders. For you there's a far more
direct version:

**3a. Subcontract from agencies that are already winning the deals.**
B Squared AI already proved this works — you built lead gen, omni-channel, CRM, LinkedIn
automation and voice agents for an agency. Find 10 more. Every AI automation agency and
GHL reseller has more sold work than delivery capacity. That is a pipeline with no
marketing cost and no audience requirement.

**3b. Partner ecosystems.** n8n, Clay, GoHighLevel, Make — they all maintain expert and
partner directories. Workflows.io is listed in HeyReach's expert directory; that listing
is itself a lead channel. Get listed where your tools list people.

**3c. Complementary services.** Web design agencies, recruiters, fractional COOs — they
all have clients who need automation and can't deliver it. Simple two-way referral, 10–15%.

**3d. Co-marketing** (his version) — do this *last*, once you have LinkedIn traction.
Nobody co-markets with an account at 365 followers.

**No.** Not at $5.5–8.5K/month. He spends more per month testing ads than you earn, and
he still can't say they work. Revisit at $20K/month, and only to amplify a post that
already worked organically.

## PART 5 — THE 90-DAY SEQUENCE

### Days 1–30: Foundation (outbound + rebuild the front door)

- [ ] LinkedIn profile rewritten as a landing page: headline states the outcome you
      produce, not your job title. Website in the headline, like he does.
- [ ] Dream 50 list built (Apollo), stakeholders mapped
- [ ] **60 min/day outbound block starts day 1.** Track: sent / replied / booked.
- [ ] 2 LinkedIn posts/week from `ALL_60_SCRIPTS.md`
- [ ] One lead magnet from `lead_magnets/` moved behind an email capture on
      webaiautomations.com
- [ ] Upwork proposal volume unchanged

**Target: 5–8 conversations, 1–2 new projects from outbound.**

### Days 31–60: Compounding (same channels, more volume)

- [ ] Posts: 2/week → **3/week**. No new channels. This is the discipline from his
      July→October stretch.
- [ ] Dream 50 → Dream 150
- [ ] 5 agency subcontracting conversations opened (Channel 3a)
- [ ] `blog_engine` running daily; one post/week manually reviewed and promoted on LinkedIn
- [ ] Qualification v1 live: Tally form → email capture → calendar only if qualified.
      Tally + Calendly + a Google Sheet. **Do not buy Clay or HubSpot yet.**

**Target: first project that came from content, not a marketplace.**

### Days 61–90: Conversion (the part most people skip)

- [ ] Posts: 3/week → **4/week**
- [ ] Pre-frame sequence built — **3 emails, not 6**: confirmation + resources,
      biggest case study, objection handling. Scale up only if show rates are poor.
- [ ] Newsletter launched: 1 email/week, fixed 4-section format. He credits his
      newsletter heavily and it costs almost nothing.
- [ ] Partnership #1 signed with referral terms in writing
- [ ] Measure: what % of this month's revenue did *not* come from Upwork?

**Target: $10K/month, with ≥30% originating outside Upwork.**

## PART 6 — THE ONE METRIC

He tracks revenue. At your stage that's the wrong number, because Upwork can produce a
good revenue month while your actual business gets more fragile.

**Track this instead, monthly:**

```
Owned Pipeline Ratio = (revenue from outbound + content + partnerships) / total revenue
```

| Month | Target |
|---|---|
| 3 | 20–30% |
| 6 | 40% |
| 12 | 60%+ |

Revenue can stay flat while this number climbs. That is still a win — it's the same thing
he's describing between January and June, just honestly labelled.

## PART 7 — THE HONEST SUMMARY

**What he's doing:** building an audience first, giving away all the knowledge, selling the
implementation, running one channel until it compounds before adding another, and turning
every employee into a distribution channel.

**What you should take:** the manual outbound motion (works at zero audience, pays in
weeks), the funnel-aware content structure, the "give away the what" positioning, the
discipline of raising cadence instead of adding channels, and automated qualification —
built cheap, not with his enterprise stack.

**What you should ignore:** the revenue number, the ads channel, the 6-email sequence,
and the implication that any of this happened in 12 months from nothing.

**The one line that matters:** he owns his distribution and you rent yours. Everything in
this document is about closing that gap without breaking the Upwork income that's paying
for the attempt.

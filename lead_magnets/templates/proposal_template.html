The 4-Line Proposal Template That Gets Replies

By Madhu Tarun Sai — @taruns_ai | webaiautomations.com

Most freelancers write proposals that are too long, too generic, and too focused on themselves. Clients on Upwork receive 20-50 proposals per job. They spend about 10 seconds on each. This template is designed to survive those 10 seconds and get a reply. It has landed me 16 projects with a 100% Job Success Score.

The 4-Line Formula

Line 1: [SPECIFIC OBSERVATION about their business/problem — proves you read the post]

Line 2: [RELEVANT EXPERIENCE — one sentence, one result, with a number]

Line 3: [EXACTLY WHAT YOU WILL DO — specific deliverable, not vague promises]

Line 4: [LOW-FRICTION CTA — make it easy to say yes to a small next step]

Line-by-Line Breakdown: Why Each Line Works

Line 1: The Specific Observation

What it does: Proves you actually read their job post. This is the line that separates you from the 40 copy-paste proposals they received.

How to write it: Reference something specific from their post, their website, or their business. Name the tool they mentioned. Quote a phrase they used. Point out a problem they described.

Template:

I noticed you're [SPECIFIC DETAIL FROM THEIR POST] — [YOUR OBSERVATION ABOUT WHY THAT'S A PROBLEM/OPPORTUNITY].

Examples:

  • "I noticed you're manually screening candidates across 3 ATS systems — that's exactly the kind of bottleneck where 10+ hours/week disappear."
  • "I saw you're using Go High Level but struggling with lead routing — the sub-account structure makes this tricky without custom API work."
  • "Your post mentions you're spending $5K/month on ads with inconsistent lead quality — I've solved this exact problem for a healthcare client."
Never open with: "Dear Hiring Manager," "I am a skilled freelancer," "I am excited to apply," "I have X years of experience." These are instant deletes.

Line 2: The Relevant Experience

What it does: Establishes credibility with ONE proof point. Not your life story. Not your resume. One relevant result.

How to write it: Pick the result from your experience that is closest to their problem. Include a number. Be specific about the industry if it matches.

Template:

I recently [BUILT/AUTOMATED/DESIGNED] [SPECIFIC SYSTEM] for a [SIMILAR CLIENT TYPE] — [ONE MEASURABLE RESULT].

Examples:

  • "I recently built an automated screening pipeline for a staffing firm with 15 offices — it cut their hiring time from 6 weeks to under 1 week."
  • "I built a 60-node n8n workflow for a construction company that manages 14,000 leads across 10 sub-accounts."
  • "I set up an email automation system for a healthcare client that gets 93% open rates and reduced their ad spend by 70%."

Line 3: The Specific Deliverable

What it does: Tells them exactly what they will get. Not "I can help you." Not "I have ideas." A concrete deliverable.

How to write it: Describe the system, the tools, and the outcome in one sentence. Make it sound like something that already exists in your head.

Template:

For your project, I'd build [SPECIFIC SYSTEM] using [TOOLS] that [SPECIFIC OUTCOME THEY CARE ABOUT].

Examples:

  • "For your project, I'd build an automated candidate intake and scoring system using Airtable + n8n that screens, ranks, and routes applicants to the right recruiter without any manual data entry."
  • "I'd set up a CRM pipeline in Go High Level with automated follow-ups, lead scoring, and a dashboard so you can see exactly which leads are hot."
  • "I'd build a content automation system that turns your weekly voice memo into 10 LinkedIn posts and 2 blog articles, matched to your brand voice."

Line 4: The Low-Friction CTA

What it does: Makes it easy to say yes. You are not asking them to hire you. You are asking for a conversation or offering free value.

How to write it: Offer something specific and small: a free Loom video, a quick call, or a free audit. Remove all risk from the next step.

Template:

Want me to [SMALL FREE ACTION]? Happy to [EASY NEXT STEP] — no obligation.

Examples:

  • "Want me to record a quick 3-minute Loom walking through exactly how I'd set this up? No obligation — just wanted to show you the approach."
  • "I can put together a free workflow diagram showing the automation structure. Want me to send it over?"
  • "Happy to hop on a 15-minute call this week to map out the project — I'll come prepared with a rough scope."
Why 4 lines works: It takes 10-15 seconds to read. It proves you read the post (Line 1), shows you can deliver (Line 2), tells them what they get (Line 3), and makes it easy to reply (Line 4). Nothing else is needed.

When to Include a Video (Always) and How

The rule: Always include a video. Always. A 2-3 minute Loom or Vidyard video attached to your proposal increases response rates by 3-5x. It proves you are a real person who actually looked at their project.

What to include in the video:

  1. First 15 seconds: Say their name and company. Reference the specific project. ("Hi [Name], I saw your post about automating candidate screening for [Company] and I wanted to show you exactly how I'd approach this.")
  2. Next 60-90 seconds: Share your screen. Show a similar project you've built, a workflow diagram, or a quick mockup. Walk them through it. ("Here's a system I built for a similar staffing company. You can see the automation starts when a resume hits the intake form...")
  3. Last 15 seconds: CTA. ("If this looks like the right approach, let's hop on a quick 15-minute call to map it out for your specific workflow. Looking forward to hearing from you.")

Video tips:

  • Keep it under 3 minutes. Nobody watches a 10-minute proposal video.
  • Use Vidyard (free) or Loom (free). Both give you a trackable link so you know if they watched it.
  • Clean your desktop before recording. Close Slack, email, and anything personal.
  • Look at the camera when you talk. Share your screen only when showing work.
  • Do not script it word-for-word. A slightly imperfect, genuine video beats a polished robotic one.
Time investment: A custom 3-minute video takes about 10 minutes total (prep + record + upload). If you send 5 proposals per day, that's 50 minutes on videos. That 50 minutes is the difference between a 5% response rate and a 20% response rate. Do the math.

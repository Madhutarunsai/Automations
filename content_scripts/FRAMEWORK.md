# Script Rewrite Framework: Andrew Scarpati Method

## What Was Wrong With the Original Scripts

After analyzing all 35 scripts, here are the recurring problems:

### 1. Hooks Are Telling, Not Showing
- Original: "I was an English teacher in Thailand six months ago. Now I build AI systems for Fortune 500 companies."
- Problem: Abstract. No image. "English teacher" is generic.
- Fix: Give a specific scene — "Six months ago I was teaching 7-year-olds the alphabet in a classroom in Bangkok for $800 a month..."

### 2. Stories Lack Specific Moments
- Original: "A staffing company was spending 6 weeks to fill one job."
- Problem: No name. No scene. No character to root for.
- Fix: "Maria owns a staffing agency in Dallas. Five recruiters. Every month she'd lose 2-3 clients because her team took 6 weeks to fill a role..."

### 3. Numbers Without Emotional Stakes
- Original: "Qualified rate went from 40% to 78%."
- Problem: Stats with no context. Why should viewer care?
- Fix: "Maria's team used to reject 6 out of 10 candidates for being wrong fits. Now it's 2 out of 10. She fired nobody. Her team just stopped wasting time."

### 4. Vague CTAs
- Original: "Follow for more."
- Problem: No reason why. No urgency.
- Fix: "Follow @taruns_ai — I'm posting my entire playbook this month. Miss it and you restart from scratch."

### 5. Generic Pain Points
- Original: "If your team is doing manual work..."
- Problem: Not visceral. Not specific.
- Fix: "Check your calendar. How many hours last week did you spend copy-pasting between tabs? That's the work a robot does while you sleep."

## The Scarpati Formula (What Top Creators Actually Do)

Every 60-second script follows this structure:

```
HOOK (0-3s): Pattern interrupt + specific scene + cliffhanger
SETUP (3-10s): Name the enemy / identify the lie / show the problem
STORY (10-40s): ONE specific story with a name, a moment, a transformation
LESSON (40-50s): The simple takeaway in 1 sentence
CTA (50-60s): Ask for ONE thing. Make it feel urgent.
```

### Hook Patterns That Stop the Scroll

1. **Specific number + unusual contrast**: "I made $47 in my first month freelancing. Here's the email that 10x'd me."
2. **Confession**: "I almost quit freelancing 4 times. This is what made me stay."
3. **Callout the lie**: "Everyone tells you to pick a niche. That's the wrong advice."
4. **The weird detail**: "The reason I got my first client wasn't my skills. It was a 90-second video."
5. **Second-person specific**: "If you've sent 50 Upwork proposals and gotten 0 replies — here's why."

### Simple English Rules

- No word longer than 3 syllables unless absolutely needed
- Short sentences. Under 12 words each.
- Use "you" 3x more than "I"
- Replace business words with street words:
  - "Utilize" → "use"
  - "Implement" → "build"
  - "Leverage" → "turn into"
  - "Demonstrate" → "show"
  - "Optimize" → "make better"
- Read every line out loud. If you'd never say it, rewrite it.

### Emotional Connection Rules

- Every script needs ONE real person (name them if possible)
- Every story needs ONE specific moment (where they were, what they felt)
- Every lesson needs ONE clear enemy (the lie, the mistake, the trap)
- End with ONE specific next step (not "learn more" — "DM me WORD")

## Before vs After Examples

### Script 1 Hook — AI Automation Explained

**Before (weak):**
> "I was an English teacher in Thailand six months ago. Now I build AI systems for Fortune 500 companies. Here's what AI automation actually means."

**After (Scarpati style):**
> "Six months ago I was teaching kids how to say 'apple' in a classroom in Bangkok. Last week I got paid $15,000 to build an AI system for a company you've heard of. Same brain. Same laptop. Nothing changed except this one skill."

Why it works:
- "Teaching kids how to say apple" = visual scene you can SEE
- "Bangkok" = specific place > "Thailand"
- "$15,000" = hard number > "Fortune 500"
- "a company you've heard of" = curiosity gap
- "Same brain. Same laptop." = makes viewer think "wait, that could be me"
- "this one skill" = cliffhanger

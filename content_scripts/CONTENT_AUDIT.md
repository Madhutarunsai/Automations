# Content Audit: What to Post vs What to NEVER Post

## The Filter: Before Every Script Ask These 3 Questions

1. **Would my $5,000 client screenshot this and feel weird about hiring me?**
2. **Does this make me look like a builder or a victim?**
3. **Am I sharing this to help THEM or to get sympathy for ME?**

If any answer is wrong → cut it or reframe it.

---

## RED FLAGS: Things We Should NEVER Post

### 1. The $500 Scam Story (As Written)
**Problem:** "My first client scammed me for $500" makes you sound like someone who gets taken advantage of. A CEO considering paying you $5,000 doesn't want to hire someone who got burned on a $1,000 deal. It signals: inexperience, no contracts, no vetting.

**Fix:** Don't say "scammed." Say: "My first client paid half and disappeared after delivery. That taught me to always collect upfront or use milestone payments." Now it's a LESSON, not a sad story. You look wise, not wounded.

### 2. "I Borrowed Money From My Dad"
**Problem:** This kills your authority. You're positioning yourself as an expert who builds systems for companies. "I borrowed from my dad" sounds like you couldn't afford lunch. Your audience doesn't need to know this.

**Fix:** Remove entirely. Replace with: "I was down to my last few hundred dollars." That's struggle without being pitiful.

### 3. "I Burned Money on Courses That Taught Me Nothing"
**Problem:** Your audience buys courses. Many of them. Saying courses are worthless makes them feel stupid for buying. Plus, you might sell a course someday.

**Fix:** "I invested in courses early on. Some helped. Most didn't. What actually taught me was building real projects for real clients." Now you're not bashing courses — you're saying experience > theory. Big difference.

### 4. Exact Revenue Numbers Under $3K
**Problem:** "I made $200 that month" or "barely $1,000 in 6 months" makes you sound small. Even in a "struggle to success" story, the low numbers stick in people's heads. They'll remember "$200/month" more than "$30K in 11 months."

**Fix:** Use ratios instead of small numbers. "I made less in my first 6 months of freelancing than one month of teaching." Same story. No embarrassing number attached.

### 5. "$500-$800/Month on Tools"
**Problem:** This is almost your entire old teaching salary going to subscriptions. It makes the business sound expensive and thin-margin. A beginner reading this thinks "I can't afford that."

**Fix:** Never mention the total. Instead: "I invest in premium AI tools because they pay for themselves 10x over." Or just list specific tools without the total price. Don't scare beginners.

### 6. "I Have 365 Followers"
**Problem:** Never mention low follower counts. Nobody follows a small account BECAUSE it's small. Social proof works in reverse — low numbers repel.

**Fix:** Never mention follower count until it's impressive. Focus on: "I've worked with 15 staffing firms" and "16 Upwork projects, 100% success rate." Those are the numbers that matter.

### 7. "I Almost Went Home" / "I Almost Quit"
**Problem (when overdone):** One "I almost quit" story is powerful. Five of them across your content makes you sound unstable. Your original scripts had this theme in scripts 3, 6, 15, 19, 24, 27, 29. That's SEVEN scripts about almost quitting.

**Fix:** Use the "almost quit" story ONCE. Make it the best version. Use it in ONE hero's journey script. Every other script should reference struggle briefly but focus on the SOLUTION and the RESULT.

### 8. Specific Client Details That Could Be Traced
**Problem:** "Staffing company in Washington that placed 17,000 people" — someone could Google this and figure out who it is. You said don't name clients.

**Fix:** Keep it vague: "A staffing firm on the West Coast" or "One of my staffing clients has over 50 recruiters." Enough to be impressive, not enough to identify.

### 9. "I Was Sending 10-15 Proposals a Day"
**Problem (for authority content):** This is fine for "beginner journey" content. But if you're also posting "I'm an expert who builds $14K systems," the contrast is jarring. Pick your positioning per post.

**Fix:** For authority posts → never mention proposal grinding. For journey posts → it's perfect. Don't mix them.

### 10. The $100 First Project (if that was the real number)
**Problem:** Charging $100 for a CRM build positions you at the bottom of the market. Even in a "look how far I've come" story, the $100 anchor price is damaging.

**Fix:** We corrected this — it was $1,000. But even then, lead with: "My first project was worth $1,000. I only saw half of it." The $1,000 is the anchor, not the $500 loss.

## GREEN FLAGS: Stories That BUILD Authority

### 1. The Reverse Engineering Stories ✅
"A CRM had zero API documentation. Every other freelancer gave up. I opened the browser inspector, found the hidden endpoints, and built a 60-node automation." — This is GOLD. It shows you can solve problems nobody else can.

### 2. The Healthcare Website Visitor Story ✅
"Client was burning money on ads. I showed them how to capture the people ALREADY on their website before they leave. Same clients, 30% of the ad spend." — This makes you sound like a strategist, not just a tool operator.

### 3. The North Star Metric Conversation ✅
"Client wanted 10 features. I asked one question: what's the one thing that solves everything? Answer: money. So we focused on leads first, features later." — This positions you as a CONSULTANT, not an order-taker.

### 4. The "Parents' Knee Pain" Analogy ✅
Incredible analogy. Use it everywhere. It makes complex business concepts feel human. This is your signature storytelling device.

### 5. The Speed Results ✅
"Hiring time: 6 weeks → 1 week." "93% email open rates, sometimes 98%." "Same clients, 30% of ad spend." — Hard numbers that sound impossible. People stop scrolling for impossible-sounding numbers.

### 6. The Artist/Musician Background ✅
"I draw in 8 mediums and play guitar and piano. Art taught me patience. Patience is why I can reverse-engineer a CRM with no documentation while everyone else quits." — This is unique. Nobody else in the AI automation space has this story. USE IT.

### 7. The Teaching → AI Connection ✅
"I taught 9-year-olds to write clear sentences. That's the exact skill that makes AI prompts work." — This reframes your "weakness" (teacher, not engineer) as your superpower. Very strong.

### 8. The $30K Arc ✅
"$1,000 total in my first 6 months. Then $30,000 in the next 11 months." — Use the ratio, not the small number. "I made 30x more in my second year than my entire first year combined."

### 9. The Time/Money/Effort Framework ✅
"You need two out of three: time, money, effort. If you have time and money but no effort, you'll just burn everything." — This is original philosophy. Nobody else is saying this. Lead with it.

### 10. "Just Ask How" ✅
"90% of people have the capability. They just stopped asking 'how.'" — This is your core belief. This should be the through-line of your entire brand.

## VOICE RULES

### How Tarun Actually Talks
- Casual. Sometimes curses.
- Uses analogies from art and daily life (knee pain, drawing a horse vs donkey)
- Says "dude" and "yeah" naturally
- Explains complex things simply (teaches like talking to a friend)
- Doesn't use corporate words (no "leverage," "synergy," "optimize")
- Gets passionate when talking about people not asking "how"
- Humble about wins, honest about losses
- Always brings it back to: "what's the real problem?"

### Tone Per Platform
- **Instagram Reels:** Casual, direct, short sentences, visual stories
- **LinkedIn:** Slightly more professional, still personal, longer storytelling okay
- **YouTube:** Can go deeper, more tutorial-style, show screens

### Words Tarun Uses
- "Dude"
- "Here's the thing"
- "The real problem is..."
- "Am I right?"
- "It's a piece of cake" (once the problem is clear)
- "Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3"
- "North Star metric"
- "Reverse engineer"
- "Start from the back"

### Words Tarun Should AVOID in Content
- "Leverage" (say "use")
- "Optimize" (say "make better" or "fix")
- "Synergy" (never)
- "Utilize" (say "use")
- "Game-changer" (overused)
- "Hustle" (overused, not his vibe — he's more "patient and stubborn")

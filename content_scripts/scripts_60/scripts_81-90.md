**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
Some clients want everything at once. Every week a new feature. Every call a new direction. Here's how to handle them without losing your mind or the project.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Shiny object syndrome is the number one thing that kills freelance projects. The client keeps changing scope — not because they're bad, but because they don't have a framework to prioritize. That's your job to provide.

**STORY (10–32s):**
I had a staffing client who kept asking for more. "I want to hire more people." "I want more clients." "I want to optimize the system." "Can we add this feature? And that one?" Constantly changing scope. Every week, new priorities. Here's what I do now. I say: "Yes, that's a good idea. Can I suggest something?" I wait for them to say yes. Then: "What is the North Star metric? What one thing solves all problems right now?" The answer is always money. "How do you get money? By getting clients first. I'll do what they want — but I'll always give them an option of what's more important right now. That framework saves the project and saves the relationship.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Don't fight shiny object syndrome. Redirect it. Give the client a phased framework so every idea has a place — just not all at once.

## Script 83 — Teaching | Week 8, Tuesday
**Topic:** How to structure a discovery call that closes
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** PRICING
**Duration:** 60s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
I close most of my discovery calls. Not because I'm a good salesman. Because I never sell on the call. I diagnose. Here's my exact process.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Most freelancers get on a discovery call and start listing what they can do. "I can build automations. I can set up CRMs. I can do lead gen." That's a menu, not a conversation. The client doesn't want to hear what you can do. They want to know that you understand what they need.

**STORY (10–40s):**
My discovery call has five steps. Step one — I ask them to walk me through their current process. Not what's broken. How it works right now. Step by step. This tells me more than any brief ever could. Step two — I ask "what do you think the problem is?" They always have a theory. Sometimes they're right. Sometimes they're way off. But asking makes them feel heard. Step three — I tell them what I think the real problem is. Usually different from what they said. I use their own words from step one as proof. "You mentioned your team spends three hours on screening. That's where the bottleneck is — not the job board." Step four — I frame the outcome. "If we fix this one thing, you'll go from six weeks to hire to under one week. Am I right?" They say yes. Step five — I explain phases. Phase one, what we fix now. Phase two, what comes next. Phase three, optimization. I never quote a price on the call. I send a proposal the next day with the Vidyard video walkthrough. By then they've already decided — because the call wasn't a pitch. It was a diagnosis. And people pay the person who found the problem, not the person who listed the features.

**LESSON (40–50s):**
Stop selling on discovery calls. Start diagnosing. Ask about their process. Find the real problem. Frame the outcome. The close happens because they trust you, not because you convinced them.

**Lead Magnet Keyword:** EMAILS
**Duration:** 45s

**SETUP (3–10s):**

**STORY (10–32s):**
Three rules we follow. First — the subject line is a question, not an announcement. Not "Our AI Automation Services." Instead: "Quick question about your hiring process." Questions get opened because the brain wants to answer them. Second — the first line references something real about their business. We use AI to research each prospect — their LinkedIn posts, their website, their recent hires. The icebreaker proves we looked. Third — the email is five sentences max. Problem. Proof. One-line CTA. No attachments. No calendly links in the first email. Just a reply-friendly question: "Worth a quick chat?" That's it. We tested multiple variations — different subject lines, different icebreakers, different CTAs — until we found what hit ninety-three percent. Most people send one version and wonder why it doesn't work.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Short. Personal. A question, not a pitch. Test multiple versions, not one. That's how you go from twenty percent open rates to ninety-three.

**Lead Magnet Keyword:** PRICING
**Duration:** 45s

**SETUP (3–10s):**

**STORY (10–32s):**
A client asked me "what's your rate?" I used to panic and throw out an hourly number. Twenty-three dollars an hour. They'd do the math. "So this should take ten hours... two hundred thirty dollars." Now I don't answer with a number. I answer with a question. "It depends on the scope. Can I ask — what's the result you're looking for?" They tell me. Then I frame the value. "You're spending six weeks to fill one role. That's costing you — what — two or three lost clients per month? If we cut that to one week, what's that worth to your business?" Now they're not thinking about my hourly rate. They're thinking about the twenty or thirty thousand dollars they're losing every month. My twelve hundred dollar project feels like a bargain. That's value pricing. You price the outcome, not the hours.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Never answer "what's your rate" with a number. Answer with a question about the outcome. Price the result, not the time. Clients pay more and argue less.

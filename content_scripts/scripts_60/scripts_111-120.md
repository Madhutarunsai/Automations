## Script 111 — Teaching | Week 11, Monday
**Topic:** How to send cold emails from your phone to find your first 3 niches
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** EMAILS
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
You don't need a fancy outreach tool to start. You need a phone and three niches. Here's how to find clients before you've built anything.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Most beginners think they need a website, a CRM, an automation stack, and a perfect portfolio before they reach out to anyone. That's shiny object syndrome. You need zero of that to get your first client.

**STORY (10–32s):**
Here's what I tell everyone who asks how to start. Identify three niches. Not one — three. Because you don't know which one will respond yet. Then start cold emailing from your phone. Literally your phone. Find businesses in those niches. Check their website. Check their LinkedIn. Write a short email — what problem you noticed, how you'd fix it, one line of proof. Send it. Do this ten times a day. You'll get ignored ninety percent of the time. That's normal. But within a few weeks you'll know which niche responds, what problems they actually have, and what language they use. That data is worth more than any course. Connect on LinkedIn too. Do cold calls if you can. Leverage your network — WhatsApp, email, LinkedIn. Yes, it's cringe. But it works. Until you hit ten thousand a month, your only problem is lead generation. Everything else is a distraction.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Pick three niches. Send cold emails from your phone. Ten a day. That's the starting point. No tools needed. Just effort and patience.

**CTA (40–45s):**
Comment EMAILS and I'll send you ten cold email templates — first outreach, follow-ups, and re-engagement.

## Script 113 — Teaching | Week 11, Tuesday
**Topic:** Focus on inputs, not outputs
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 30s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
Everyone asks "how do I get clients?" Wrong question. The right question is "how many proposals did I send today?"

**SETUP (3–8s):**
Beginners obsess over results. How many clients did I get this week? How much revenue this month? But you can't control results. You can only control inputs.

**STORY (8–22s):**
When I was sending ten to fifteen proposals a day for three weeks with zero replies, I could have stared at the output — zero clients — and quit. Instead I focused on the input. Did I send ten proposals today? Yes. Were they personalized? Yes. Did I include a Vidyard video? Yes. Good. Do it again tomorrow. The output came three weeks later — six jobs in two months. But only because the inputs were consistent. Your job isn't to get clients today. Your job is to send five to ten good proposals today. The clients come from the math, not from wishing.

**LESSON (22–27s):**
Control what you can — proposals sent, quality of each, consistency. The results follow the inputs. They always do.

## Script 116 — Teaching | Week 11, Wednesday
**Topic:** How to do a growth audit for any business in fifteen minutes
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
I can find the biggest bottleneck in any small business in fifteen minutes. It's almost always one of two things. Here's how I look.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Most business owners think their problems are complicated. They have a list of twenty things that need fixing. But when you look at the system as a whole, it almost always comes down to one bottleneck that's creating all the others.

**STORY (10–32s):**
My framework is simple. I ask two questions. First — what's your revenue? If it's under fifty to two hundred thousand, the problem is almost always lead generation. They don't need a better system. They need more people to know they exist. Second — how many employees? If it's twenty-five plus and things are always messy, the problem is project management, CRM, or fulfillment. The leads are coming in but the delivery is broken. That's it. Two questions. One diagnosis. Then I look at what's leaking. Where are leads going cold? Where are candidates dropping off? Where is the team spending time on manual work that a system could handle? I frame it back: "This is the problem. Because of it, you're getting this result. If we fix this one thing, you'll reach X in six months. Am I right?" Once they feel understood, the solution practically designs itself.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Under two hundred K revenue? It's lead gen. Twenty-five plus employees and messy? It's operations. Two questions. One bottleneck. Start there.

**CTA (40–45s):**

## Script 120 — Contrarian | Week 11, Sunday
**Topic:** If you don't understand systems, tools are toys
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
People learn AI tools, think they know AI, start a business, and fail. Here's why the tools aren't the problem.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
The internet is full of "learn this tool in thirty minutes" content. ChatGPT tutorials. n8n walkthroughs. Make.com templates. People finish the tutorial, update their LinkedIn bio to "AI Expert," and wonder why nobody hires them.

**STORY (10–32s):**
If you don't understand systems, tools are just toys you bought to kill boredom. Knowing how to use ChatGPT doesn't mean you can solve a business problem. Knowing how to build an n8n workflow doesn't mean you can figure out which workflow to build. The tool is the last step, not the first. My process starts with the client's problem. Walk me through how things work now. Where is time being wasted? Where is money leaking? What one fix would solve eighty percent of the issue? Once I understand the system — the human process, the data flow, the bottleneck — then I pick the tool. Sometimes it's n8n. Sometimes it's Go High Level. Sometimes it's a Google Sheet. The tool doesn't matter if you can't diagnose the problem it needs to solve. That's the difference between a tool operator and a systems thinker. One follows tutorials. The other solves problems no tutorial covers.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Learn systems, not tools. The tool changes every year. The ability to diagnose a problem and design a solution from scratch — that's the skill that actually pays.

**CTA (40–45s):**
If you've been learning tools but can't land clients — the gap isn't technical. Comment AUDIT. I'll show you what's actually missing.

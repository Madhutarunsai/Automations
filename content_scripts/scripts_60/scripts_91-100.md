## Script 93 — Teaching | Week 9, Tuesday
**Topic:** How to use the browser inspector to find any hidden API
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** TOOLS
**Duration:** 60s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
Three tools I've worked with had zero API documentation. I connected all three. Same method every time. Here's exactly how.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
DocuSign. Controlio time tracker. Lakeshore CRM. All three — no public API docs. No developer portal. No support team that could help. Every other freelancer who tried either gave up or told the client it couldn't be done.

**STORY (10–40s):**
The process is the same every time. Step one — open the browser developer tools. The inspector. Step two — click manually on the thing you'd normally do in the app. Add a lead. Send a message. Update a field. Step three — watch the Network tab. Every click makes an API call the browser can see. You'll see the URL it calls, the method — POST to create, GET to read, PUT to update — and the exact payload it sends. Step four — copy that payload structure. Step five — recreate it in JSON inside n8n or Make.com. Call the same endpoint programmatically. Same result as clicking manually. Step six — test rate limits. If you fire a hundred calls per second, it'll break. Add sleep nodes. Add retry logic on failure. Step seven — make it solid. Error handling on every node. I always start with the simplest tool first. With the Texas metal company project, I started with the time tracker — Controlio — before tackling the harder CRM. Because if you don't have tracking working, all the other integrations are features that don't work. I research the API before I even start the project — to know if it's feasible.

**LESSON (40–50s):**
If a tool doesn't publish an API, it still has one. The browser inspector shows you every call the app makes. Copy the call. Recreate it. Automate it. That's reverse engineering.

The full tool stack I use for this — browser inspector, n8n, Supabase, Claude Code — is in my freelancer tool list. Comment TOOLS and I'll send it.

## Script 94 — Teaching | Week 9, Tuesday
**Topic:** How to diagnose a client's real problem in five minutes
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
Clients almost never know what their real problem is. They tell you one thing. The truth is usually something different. Here's how I find it every time.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Most freelancers take the client's brief at face value. "We need a CRM." So they build a CRM. But the real problem was lead generation — and the CRM sits empty. You solved the wrong problem because you didn't ask the right questions.

**STORY (10–32s):**
My process is simple. First — I ask them to walk through their current system. Not what's broken. How it works right now. I listen. I don't solve yet. Second — I ask "what problem do you think you have?" They always have a theory. Third — I diagnose based on their revenue. Under fifty to two hundred thousand? It's almost always lead generation. Twenty-five plus employees but always messy? It's project management or fulfillment. Then I frame it back: "This is your problem. Because of it, you're getting this result. If we fix this one thing, you'll reach X in six months. Am I right?" Once they feel understood — once the problem is clear — then it's a piece of cake. The solution designs itself when the diagnosis is right.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Work on the problem first. Never the solution. Understand before you build. The client pays for the diagnosis, not the features.

**CTA (40–45s):**

## Script 95 — Teaching | Week 9, Wednesday
**Topic:** The parents' knee pain analogy — how to sell without selling
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
I never pitch. I never sell. But clients say yes anyway. Here's the analogy that changed how I think about every conversation.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Most freelancers show the solution first. "I can build you this system." The client says "don't waste money" and moves on. You're leading with shoes when they don't think they have a problem.

**STORY (10–32s):**
Think about your parents. They're fifty. They have knee pain. They think it's fine. They keep walking on it. You know it's damaging their knees long-term. If you show them expensive shoes first, they'll say "don't waste your money." But if you explain what happens if they keep walking on a bad knee — the damage, the surgery, the years of pain — now they understand the problem. Now when you show them the shoes, they say "where do I buy them?" Same thing in business. I never lead with what I can build. I lead with what's breaking. "You're spending six weeks to fill one role. That's costing you clients every month. Your qualified candidate rate is below fifty percent. Here's what that looks like in twelve months if nothing changes." Once they see the damage — then the solution sells itself. Show the problem before the solution. Every time.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Don't sell solutions. Sell the cost of the problem. When the client understands what they're losing, they buy the fix without being sold.

**CTA (40–45s):**
If you suspect something is broken but you're not sure what it's costing you — comment AUDIT. I'll show you the damage in fifteen minutes.

## Script 96 — Teaching | Week 9, Wednesday
**Topic:** How to go from twenty-three dollars an hour to value pricing
**Lead Magnet Keyword:** PRICING
**Duration:** 45s

**HOOK (0–3s):**
I used to make twenty-three dollars an hour on Upwork. Now my smallest project is twelve hundred dollars. I didn't get better at the work. I got better at framing the value.

**SETUP (3–10s):**
Hourly pricing punishes you for being fast. The better you get, the less you earn per project. That's backwards. Value pricing flips it — you charge for the result, not the time.

**STORY (10–32s):**
Early on, clients would ask my rate. I'd say twenty-three an hour. They'd estimate the hours, multiply, and land on two hundred to four hundred dollars for the project. I was doing the same quality work I do now — just framed wrong. The shift happened when I stopped answering "what's your rate" with a number. Instead I'd ask: "What result are you looking for?" Then I'd frame what that result is worth to their business. A staffing firm losing clients because hiring takes six weeks? That's tens of thousands in lost revenue per quarter. My twelve hundred dollar project to fix that? That's not an expense. That's a rounding error compared to what they're losing. Projects went from two hundred dollars to twelve hundred. Then to six thousand. Same skills. Same tools. Different conversation.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Stop pricing your time. Start pricing the outcome. Ask what the result is worth. Then price a fraction of that. The client pays more and feels like they got a deal.

**Lead Magnet Keyword:** AUDIT
**Duration:** 45s

YouTube course graduates can copy a system. Ask them to build something new and they freeze. Here's the difference between copying and actually knowing what you're doing.

**SETUP (3–10s):**

**STORY (10–32s):**
It's like drawing art. You can copy a portrait by looking at it. Every line is right there. You trace the shapes, match the shadows, and it looks great. But when someone asks you to draw a completely different species — a horse, a donkey, something you've never seen — how will you do it? You need to have drawn a horse first. Understood how the body works. How the muscles connect. How the legs bend. Then with your imagination, you can draw the donkey. That's the difference between tutorial builders and real builders. Tutorial people learn tools — ChatGPT, n8n, Make.com — and think they can solve any problem. Real builders understand the problem first, then pick the tool. The tool doesn't matter if you don't understand what you're building or why.

**LESSON (32–40s):**
Don't learn tools. Learn how to think about problems. Tools change every year. The ability to diagnose, reverse engineer, and build from scratch — that stays.
